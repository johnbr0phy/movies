# BARNEY: beat sheet

*A short film with no dialogue, about 3 minutes 30 seconds long. A small boy carries a goldfish home through a world that will not keep still.*

## The idea in one line

Barney carries a goldfish in a plastic bag through a world where every surface can be a floor. By the end we find out who has been carrying whom.

## The two gravities (the grammar of the film)

The confusion has rules, so the audience can learn them and be surprised by them in turn.

1. **Barney's down is whatever he is standing on.** He walks up walls, across ceilings and along the faces of skyscrapers without noticing anything odd. His hair and his cape fall towards his own feet.
2. **The water in the bag always knows the true down.** Whatever the camera and Barney are doing, the water sits level with the real world. It is the audience's compass. When the water line is sideways in the frame, the picture is lying to us.
3. **The camera takes Barney's side late.** It rolls to adopt his new down a beat after he does, so for a moment we see him standing sideways.
4. **Stripes and pleats measure space.** Every surface is ruled with Breton stripes or pressed pleats, so when space bends, the lines show the bend.
5. **His footsteps never lie.** Two steps a second, in every set and at every angle. The score is in 7/8 against them, so the music limps and the boy doesn't.

## Beats

| # | Beat | What happens | What it overturns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | An extreme close-up of a goldfish in a plastic bag. The water line runs diagonally across the frame. We pull back and roll: a boy is walking along the ceiling of a white corridor, the bag hanging towards the ceiling, the water pooled in its knotted neck. | Which way is up. |
| 2 | **Title** | BARNEY in striped Didone capitals. The letters fall upwards, one at a time. | Letters that fall. |
| 3 | **The corridor** | A symmetrical Breton-striped corridor. Barney walks towards us and gets *smaller* with every step. | Perspective. |
| 4 | **The corridor, side on** | The corridor is a funnel, an Ames room. He is his normal size at the narrow end, beside a small door. | Our eyes. |
| 5 | **The door** | He opens it. Beyond the door, a chequered plaza stands on its edge, its horizon vertical. | Doors lead to rooms. |
| 6 | **Through** | He steps through and stands sideways on the plaza. The camera rolls ninety degrees to agree with him. The water in the bag stays level with the old world. | Floors are floors. |
| 7 | **The plaza** | He walks at a striped wall and straight up it without breaking stride. Across the square, a pleated figure strolls upside down along an arcade. | Walls. |
| 8 | **The ceiling** | Upside down under the arcade, Barney grins at his fish. The fish, right way up for us, swims upside down for him. | Who is upside down. |
| 9 | **The Pleats** | A procession of tall, flat, pleated paper people in lemon, cobalt, fuchsia and tangerine walks a pale runway in lockstep. Barney falls into step. | People. |
| 10 | **Edge on** | The camera orbits. The Pleats vanish into lines one by one, because they are paper. One folds itself into a crane and flies away. | Bodies have depth. |
| 11 | **The stairs** | An isometric staircase. Barney climbs four flights and arrives where he began. He looks at us. He turns and walks down, and rises. The music is a Shepard scale that climbs forever. | Stairs go somewhere. |
| 12 | **Off axis** | The camera slides off the magic angle. The stairs are broken pieces floating at different distances, and Barney is standing at a gap. He jumps it. | The trick is revealed, and he doesn't care. |
| 13 | **The slide** | A pleated tube slide in a playground of cones. Barney sits at the bottom. | |
| 14 | **Up the slide** | He slides *up*, fast and delighted, the bag held high. The sound plays backwards. The slide keeps going into the sky and throws him out. | Slides. |
| 15 | **The city** | A vertical canyon of towers with layers of flying traffic. Barney lands on the face of a tower and stands on it as though it were pavement. | |
| 16 | **The facade** | He walks down the windows. Cars pass above and below him. A billboard shows a giant goldfish. | Streets are horizontal. |
| 17 | **The puncture** | The fin of an orange taxi nicks the bag. A drop squirts out and falls along the facade, towards the true down. He pinches the hole. It still drips. | Stakes. |
| 18 | **Grey** | A vast grey pleated plain under a flat grey sky. Only the fish is orange. The drips leave dark spots behind him. The music stops. | Colour. |
| 19 | **The low point** | He sits. The bag is half empty and the fish is cramped. A drop falls from the bag, *upwards*, into the sky. He watches it go. | Gravity again. The water knows something. |
| 20 | **The turn** | He follows the drops. As he walks, the horizon tips and rolls over, and the grey sky turns out to be deep blue water. The sky was the sea. | The sky. |
| 21 | **The shore overhead** | Barney stands on the sky with the sea hanging over him like a ceiling of waves. He kneels at its surface and unties the bag. | |
| 22 | **Release** | The goldfish slips into the sea, turns and looks back. Then thousands of goldfish bloom in the blue. The motif plays whole for the only time, right way up and upside down at once. | The fish was never alone. |
| 23 | **Goodbye** | The fish touches its nose to the surface where his hand is, and swims away. Barney holds the empty bag. | |
| 24 | **The pull-back** | One continuous move. We rise with the fish through the sea, and Barney's whole world (the sky, the city, the stairs, the plaza) resolves into a glass bowl sitting on the sea floor. | The world. |
| 25 | **The tap** | The goldfish, outside the glass, looks in. Tiny Barney walks up the curved inside of the bowl and taps the glass. The fish taps back. Its eye fills the frame with Barney reflected upside down in it. Cut to black on the second tap. | Who was carrying whom. |
| 26 | **Credits** | White paper and stripes. Barney walks along the lines of the credits and over the edges of the frame. | |

## Why this ending

The film opens on a fish seen through plastic by a boy and closes on a boy seen through glass by a fish. Everything Barney did was to get the fish home. The last surprise is that he was already inside someone else's care. It turns the whole film over one last time, and it is the only turn that is about feeling rather than geometry.
